Portuguese cannot start a word with s and another consonant.
Three different histories all end in es-.

Twenty-eight cards carry a word that starts with es and a consonant. Sixteen grew that e, because the word was hard to say without it. Nine gained nothing. They began with a longer piece, and that piece wore away until only es was left. Two hold one Basque word that arrived with es already there. One is still argued over. On paper all twenty-eight look alike.

Ten more words belong on this page and are not in your deck. They are marked no card and they are all A1 to B1. Four of them are A1, so they are worth adding today.

Prosthesis — an extra sound put at the front of a word only to make it easier to say. It carries no meaning of its own. The e in escola is one.

Prefix — a piece put at the front that does carry meaning. Latin ex- meant “out” and abs- meant “away”. In obscūrum the prefix is ob- “over”, and the s after it belongs to the next piece. The front of the word wears down the same way in all three.

Starred form — an old word or root with a * in front of it, like *exfricāre. Nobody ever wrote that form down. Scholars work it back from the words we do have. It is a good guess, not a record.

Put the Latin pieces back, and an English relative appears Then the es is a worn prefix, not a helper. Each row prints the two Latin pieces.

English once had the e too — the English word strange came from Old French estrange. That is extrāneum after exactly the same wearing down. English later dropped the e and kept the rest. Portuguese estranho and English strange are the same word, and your card 0507 is the older-looking of the two.

Five things this layer found in your own cards

Topic levelA1— 5 findings, all about A1 or A2 words. Read this when you next edit your deck.

estação and estação do ano are one Latin word

Card 0291 is estação “station”. Card 0646 is estação do ano “season”. Both come from Latin statiōnem, “a standing, a stopping place”. A season is where the year stops for a while. Studied apart, the two look unrelated.

Suggested edit: cross-reference the two cards, and note the shared root with estar (0078).

esticar cannot be placed

Card 0080. Sources do not agree on where this word comes from. One traces it to Old French estrikier and a Germanic verb “to stretch”, which would put the e on the added road. Another calls the origin obscure and stops there. It is the only card on the page that cannot be put on any road, so it is printed on its own with the disagreement marked.

No edit suggested. The card is fine; the history is not settled.

Suggested cards: espelho (m, -s): mirror, escada (f, -s): staircase, estrada (f, -s): road, estudante (m/f, -s): student

espelho is the one to add first

Two layers changed it, like escolher and estranho above. espelho shows both changes most clearly. Latin speculum gained an e at the front. That is this page. Then it lost its u, so c and l met and became lh. That is layer 04. The path is speculum → especulum → espec'lum → espelho, and the apostrophe stands for the lost u.

Suggested card: espelho (m, -s): mirror — Eu vejo-me no espelho.

About the level flags. A1 A2 B1 B2 C1 are CEFR levels. Your deck stores no level for nouns, adjectives or phrases. It does mark 145 verb cards (A1), but that is the level of the course book they were taken from, not a judgement about each word — it covers mergulhar “to dive” as readily as falar. So the flags here are still an estimate, and a few of them sit above that mark on purpose. They follow how common the word is in everyday European Portuguese and where it normally appears in A1–B1 course books. Treat them as a reading order, not as a rule. If a word feels easy and it says B1, learn it.

Where the origin is not firm. Six rows carry a dashed edge. One is marked sources disagree: esticar cannot be placed on either road. Five are marked starred form: escolher, escolha, esquecer-se de, esfregar and espantado start from Latin forms that were never written down. Scholars work those back from the modern words, and a star marks a form like that. A starred form is a good guess, not a record. Five of the six rows are cards; escolha is one of the ten words with no card. A row with a grey background but no dashed edge is not in doubt: the grey only says the row carries a note under it.
