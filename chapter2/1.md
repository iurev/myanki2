**1. Grammar**

*   **Present Continuous Tense:** The structure `estar (conjugated) + a + infinitive` of the main verb is used to talk about an ongoing action.
    *   **Translation:** to be doing something
    *   **Example from the text:**
        *   `Ele está a ver o mar.` (He is watching the sea.)
        *   `A prima Antónia está a cozinhar.` (Cousin Antónia is cooking.)
        *   `Eu estou a comer.` (I am eating.)

@@@

*   **Forming Yes/No Questions:** A yes-no question follows the same structure as an affirmative sentence. You simply need to change the intonation.
    *   **Example from the text:**
        *   Affirmative: `Tu estás com fome.` (You are hungry.)
        *   Question: `Tu estás com fome?` (Are you hungry?)

@@@

*   **Regular Verbs (Present Tense, -ar):** Verbs that follow a specific conjugation pattern. To conjugate, remove the `-ar` ending from the infinitive and add the appropriate ending for the personal pronoun.
    *   **`chamar` (to call)**
        *   `eu chamo` (I call)
        *   `tu chamas` (you call)
        *   `ele chama` (he calls)
        *   `nós chamamos` (we call)
        *   `vocês chamam` (you call)
        *   `eles chamam` (they call)
    *   **`falar` (to speak)**
        *   **Example from the text:** `Ele fala português.` (He speaks Portuguese.)
    *   **`comprar` (to buy)**
        *   **Example from the text:** `Ele compra vinho.` (He buys wine.)

@@@

*   **Irregular Verbs (Present Tense):**
    *   **`ver` (to see, to watch)**
        *   `eu vejo` (I see)
        *   `tu vês` (you see)
        *   `ele vê` (he sees)
        *   `nós vemos` (we see)
        *   `vocês veem` (you see)
        *   `eles veem` (they see)
    *   **Example from the text:** `Eu não vejo nada.` (I don’t see anything.)

@@@

*   **Prepositions:**
    *   **`para`**: Indicates direction.
        *   **Translation:** to, at
        *   **Example from the text:** `O Pedro olha para a Maria.` (Pedro looks at Maria.)

@@@

*   **Articles (Definite and Indefinite):**
    *   **Definite Articles:** Used to refer to specific nouns.
        *   Masculine: `o` (singular), `os` (plural)
        *   Feminine: `a` (singular), `as` (plural)
        *   **Example from the text:** `o mar` (the sea), `a prima` (the cousin)
    *   **Indefinite Articles:** Refers to something in generic terms.
        *   Masculine: `um` (singular), `uns` (plural)
        *   Feminine: `uma` (singular), `umas` (plural)
        *   **Example from the text:** `Uma coisa no mar.` (Something (a thing) in the sea.), `Uma ilha?` (An island?)

@@@

*   **Negation and Adverbs:**
    *   **`nada`**: Used in a negative sentence and placed after the verb.
        *   **Translation:** nothing, anything
        *   **Example from the text:** `Eu não vejo nada.` (I don’t see anything.)
    *   **`também`**: Placed between the personal pronoun and the verb.
        *   **Translation:** also, as well
        *   **Example from the text:** `Eu também sou professor.` (I am also a teacher.)
    *   **`também não`**:
        *   **Translation:** also not, neither
        *   **Example from the text:** `Agora o João também não vê a coisa.` (Now João also doesn’t see the thing.)
