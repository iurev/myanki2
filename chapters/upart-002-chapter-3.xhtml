“Yes, correct.”

* * *

NOTES

— Boa noite (1). Eu tenho (2) uma reserva.

“Good evening. I have a reservation.”

(1)

The three expressions below are used to greet and to say goodbye, depending on the time of day.

  • bom dia: good morning (from around sunrise to noon)
  • boa tarde: good afternoon (from noon to 8 pm)
  • boa noite: good evening (from around 8 pm onwards), good night

You can use them to greet in a formal way, or you can use them together with olá for a semi-formal greeting.

(at the store)

  • — Bom dia. Good morning.

(with the neighbor)

  • — Olá, bom dia! Hi, good morning!

‎(2)

The verb ter (to have) is an irregular verb used to express possession.

  • Eu tenho uma coisa. I have a thing.

‎conjugation of the verb ‘ter’ (present)

  • eu tenho (I have)
  • tu tens (you have)
  • ele tem (he has)
  • nós temos (we have)
  • vocês têm (you have)
  • eles têm (they have)

— Boa noite. Qual é o nome? (3)

“Good evening. What is the name?”

(3)

Qual é o nome?: What (which) is the name?

To build an open question, you need an interrogative pronoun. You have seen onde (where) before in the story. The interrogative pronoun is placed at the beginning of the question followed by the verb.

  • Onde está o João? Where is João?

This section of the story includes a new interrogative pronoun: qual (which). It is commonly used with the verb ser.

  • Qual é a casa? What (which) is the house?

When referring to a plural object, you use the plural interrogative pronoun quais. Note that the verb conjugation changes accordingly.

  • Quais são os livros? What (which) are the books?

— Um momento, por favor (4). Um quarto para (5) duas (6) pessoas?

“One moment, please. A room for two (people)?”

(4)

The expression por favor (or: se faz favor) means please‎.

  • Um café, por favor. A coffee, please.

(5)

You have seen before that you can use the preposition para with the verb olhar (olhar para: to look at) to express direction. This preposition can be used in a variety of different situations. In this section of the story the preposition para means for, identifying the recipient of something.

You can see in the examples below that, regardless of what the English translation of the preposition is, it always expresses the general meaning of direction or destination.

  • Eu olho para a Andreia. I look at Andreia. > Andreia is the recipient of my look.
  • Eu tenho uma mala para o Pedro. I have a suitcase for Pedro. > Pedro is the recipient of the suitcase.
  • Eu vou para o Porto. I go to Oporto. > Oporto is the ‘recipient’ of me.

(6)

You’ve seen before that um and uma are the masculine and feminine indefinite articles. They mean a/an in English (um carro: a car).

Um and uma also mean one.

  • masculine: um carro (a car, one car)
  • feminine: uma cadeira (a chair, one chair)

‎The quantity two (2), also changes according to gender.

  • masculine: dois carros (two cars)
  • feminine: duas cadeiras (two chairs)

Other numbers/quantities are invariable (3, 4, 5, etc.).

  • três carros (three cars)
  • três cadeiras (three chairs)

— Ah, sim. Desculpe (7).

“Oh, yes. Sorry.”

(7)

This is the verb desculpar (to apologize) conjugated in the imperative. Learning these two words below in the imperative is enough for now.

  • ‎desculpe: sorry (formal)
  • desculpa: sorry (informal)

Note that the only difference between the words is the last vowel.

(at the restaurant)

  • — A sopa não é para mim. The soup is not for me.
  • — Desculpe! Sorry.

(with a friend)

  • — Estás atrasado! You are late!
  • — Desculpa. Sorry.

— A senhora (8) tem uma mala?

“Do you have a suitcase?”

(8)

You have seen before that tu (you) is what you use to address someone informally. To talk to someone formally, you can use:

  • a senhora: used to talk to a woman in a formal way
  • o senhor: used to talk to a man in a formal way

Remember that ele (he), ela (she) have the same verb conjugation as the formal treatment (third-person singular).

Compare:

  • O senhor tem uma reserva? Do you (man) have a reservation? / Does he have a reservation?
  • A senhora tem uma reserva? Do you (woman) have a reservation? / Does she have a reservation?
  • Ele tem uma reserva? Does he have a reservation?
  • Ela tem uma reserva? Does she have a reservation?

Senhora (or the abbreviation Sra.) can also mean Mrs.

Senhor (or the abbreviation Sr.) can also mean Mr.

  • Onde está a Sra. Ana Moreira? Where is Mrs. Ana Moreira?
  • Onde está o Sr. José Pacheco? Where is Mr. José Pacheco?

— Muito (9) obrigada (10).

“Thank you very much.”

(9)

The word muito means very. It is invariable when placed before an adjective, i.e., it doesn’t change its form depending on the gender and number of the adjective.

  • Ela é muito simpática. She is very nice.
  • Ele é muito alto. He is very tall.

(10)

There are two ways to say thank you, depending on whether you are a man or a woman.

  • obrigado: thank you (said by a man)
  • obrigada: thank you (said by a woman)

‎Muito obrigada means thank you very much.

She looks at the bag’s zipper. It’s broken.

* * *

NOTES

Está muito cansada (1).

(She) is very tired.

(1)

dropping the personal pronoun

You have seen until now the subject at the beginning of each sentence (Eu sou biólogo. I am a biologist.).

In Portuguese, you can leave out the subject pronoun when it is clear who you are talking about. Usually, the verb conjugation is enough to know who the sentence refers to.

In this story section, the first sentence starts by identifying the person (Marina). For this reason, it’s not necessary to repeat the subject again in the following sentence.

There is no set rule about this, i.e. it’s not incorrect to include the pronoun. However, repeating the pronoun at the beginning of each sentence can sound unnatural.

  • A Ana está em casa. (Ela) está a jantar. Ana is at home. She is having dinner.

Ela quer tomar (2) um duche.

She wants to take a shower.

(2)

querer (to want) + infinitive: to want to do something

The structure querer (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb means to want to do something. The verb querer (to want) is an irregular verb.

  • Ele quer estar em casa. He wants to be home.

conjugation of the verb ‘querer’ (present)

  • eu quero (I want)
  • tu queres (you want)
  • ele quer (he wants)
  • nós queremos (we want)
  • vocês querem (you want)
  • eles querem (they want)

Ela abre (3) a mala.

She opens the suitcase.

(3)

You have learned how to conjugate regular verbs ending in -ar. In this story section, we have one verb that ends in -ir: abrir (to open).

We can see that the conjugation for the third person singular (ele, ela) ends in -e:

  • A Marina abre (Marina opens)

If I give you a new verb ending in -ir, you’ll be able to conjugate it in the third person singular.

The verb ’partir’ (to break)

  • Ele parte o copo. He breaks the glass.

The verb ’discutir’ (to argue)

  • Ele discute com o amigo. He argues with his (the) friend.

Here are the endings for each personal pronoun after you remove the ending from the infinitive.

conjugation of the verb ‘abrir’ (present)

  • eu abro (I open)
  • tu abres (you open)
  • ele abre (he opens)
  • nós abrimos (we open)
  • vocês abrem (you open)
  • eles abrem (they open)
