NOTES

— Bom dia. Aqui está o barco. — diz (1) o pescador.

“Good Morning. Here’s the boat,” says the fisherman.

(1)

The verb dizer (to say, to tell) is irregular. The third-person singular ends in -z. This happens with other verbs with z before the infinitive ending (fazer: to do, to make / conduzir: to drive).

  • Ele diz a verdade. He tells the truth.
  • Ela faz o almoço. She cooks (makes) lunch.

conjugation of the verb ‘dizer’ (present)

  • eu digo (I say)
  • tu dizes (you say)
  • ele diz (he says)
  • nós dizemos (we say)
  • vocês dizem (you say)
  • eles dizem (they say)

conjugation of the verb ‘fazer’ (present)

  • eu faço (I do)
  • tu fazes (you do)
  • ele faz (he does)
  • nós fazemos (we do)
  • vocês fazem (you do)
  • eles fazem (they do)

— O senhor vai ao mar (2)? — pergunta o pescador.

“Are you going to sea?” asks the fisherman. (lit.: Do you go to the sea?)

(2)

ir (to go) + a + location: to go somewhere

The verb ir (to go), conjugated, together with the preposition a is used to express that you are going somewhere but not staying for long. The verb ir is an irregular verb.

conjugation of the verb ‘ir’ (present)

  • eu vou (I go)
  • tu vais (you go)
  • ele vai (he goes)
  • nós vamos (we go)
  • vocês vão (you go)
  • eles vão (they go)

As you have seen with other prepositions, the preposition a contracts with the article that follows:

  • a + o mar = ao mar (to the sea)
  • a + a praia = à praia (to the beach)
  • a + os Estados Unidos da América = aos Estados Unidos da América (to the USA)
  • a + as lojas = às lojas (to the stores)
  • Ele vai ao escritório. He goes to the office. (ao = a + o)

Summary of the contractions

  • a + o = ao
  • a + a = à
  • a + os = aos
  • a + as = às

— O senhor vai ao mar? — pergunta o pescador, incrédulo.

“Are you going to sea?” asks the fisherman, incredulous.

— Vou, vou (3).

“Yes, I am.”

(3)

Vou is the verb ir (to go) conjugated in the first person singular (eu). When you answer a question and want to emphasize your affirmative answer, it’s common to reply with the conjugated verb twice.

This can happen when the person asking the question is surprised by the information, or believes you will answer negatively.

  • Ainda queres comer mais? Do you still want to eat more?
  • Quero, quero. Yes, I do. (lit. I want, I want)

This also happens when the person asks a negative question, and you answer affirmatively.

  • Não queres comer? Don’t you want to eat?
  • Quero, quero. Yes, I do. (lit. I want, I want)

— O senhor vai pescar (4)? Aqui há (5) peixes (6)!

“Are you going fishing? There are fish here!”

(4)

ir (to go) + infinitive: event in the future

The structure ir (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used to identify an event that will take place in the future.

  • Eu vou comer. I am going to eat.
  • Tu vais comer. You are going to eat.
  • O Carlos vai comer. Carlos is going to eat.
  • Nós vamos comer. We are going to eat.
  • Vocês vão comer. You are going to eat.
  • Os alunos vão comer. The students are going to eat.

‎Note that the present tense can also be used to refer to events that will take place in the future.

  • ‎Eu estou em casa amanhã. I am home tomorrow. (= I will be home tomorrow.)

‎(5)

The word há means there is or there are. Unlike in English, it doesn’t change when you refer to more than one thing.

  • Aqui há um restaurante. There is a restaurant here.
  • Aqui há dois restaurantes. There are two restaurants here.

‎(6)

You have seen that there are nouns in the singular form (when the noun refers to one thing) and in the plural form (when the noun refers to more than one thing).

To form the plural, you will usually add a letter or change the last letter of the singular word.

When the words ends in a vowel, you add an -s at the end.

  • singular: o carro (the car)
  • plural: os carros (the cars)

When the word ends in -r, you add -es to the end.

  • singular: o professor (the teacher)
  • plural: os professores (the teachers)

When the word ends in -m, you change the m to n and add -s.

  • singular: o homem (the man)
  • plural: os homens (the men)

There are other situations, but you will only learn these for now.

— Vai passear, então (7)?

“Are you going for a ride, then?”

(7)

The word então means so.

  • Então, estás cansado? So, are you tired?

— Tem a certeza (8)?

“Are you sure?”

(8)

The structure ter a certeza (to be sure) uses the verb ter (to have).

  • Tens a certeza? Are you sure?
  • Tenho, tenho. Yes, I am.

NOTES

— Aqui desaparecem (1) muitos (2) barcos de pescadores.

“Many fishing boats disappear here.”

(1)

You have learned to conjugate regular verbs ending in -ar and -ir. In this story section, we have one verb that ends in -er: desaparecer (to disappear). We can see that the conjugation for the third person plural (eles, elas, formal) ends in -em:

  • desaparecem… (boats) disappear

‎If I give you a new verb ending in -er, you’ll be able to conjugate it in the third person plural.

The verb ’comer’ (to eat)

  • Eles comem o bolo. They eat the cake.

The verb ’beber’ (to drink)

  • Eles bebem o vinho. They drink the wine.

Here are the endings for each personal pronoun after you remove the ending from the infinitive.

conjugation of the verb ‘comer’ (present)

  • eu como (I eat)
  • tu comes (you eat)
  • ele come (he eats)
  • nós comemos (we eat)
  • vocês comem (you eat)
  • eles comem (they eat)

‎(2)

You’ve learned muito (very) before. It’s invariable (it doesn’t change its form) before adverbs or adjectives.

  • Ele corre muito depressa. He runs very fast.
  • Ela é muito bonita. She is very pretty.
  • O barco é muito grande. The boat is very big.

‎muito can also be variable when placed before a noun, and it means a lot, many. The ending changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.

  • feminine, singular: A loja tem muita variedade. The shop has a lot of variety.
  • masculine, singular: O gato tem muito pelo. The cat has a lot of hair.
  • feminine, plural: A flor tem muitas cores. The flower has many colors.
  • masculine, plural: O Pedro tem muitos amigos. Pedro has many friends.

‎Summary

  • muito (masculine, singular)
  • muitos (masculine, plural)
  • muita (feminine, singular)
  • muitas (feminine, plural)

Por isso vêm (3) poucos (4) barcos aqui.

That’s why few boats come here.”

(3)

The verb vir (to come) is an irregular verb.‎

  • A encomenda vem hoje. The order comes today.

conjugation of the verb ‘vir’ (present)

  • eu venho (I come)
  • tu vens (you come)
  • ele vem (he comes)
  • nós vimos (we come)
  • vocês vêm (you come)
  • eles vêm (they come)

‎(4)

Similarly to muito (a lot, many), pouco (few, little) is variable when placed before a noun. The ending changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.

  • feminine, singular: A loja tem pouca variedade. The shop has little variety.
  • masculine, singular: O gato tem pouco pelo. The cat has little hair.
  • feminine, plural: A flor tem poucas cores. The flower has few colors.
  • masculine, plural: O Pedro tem poucos amigos. Pedro has few friends.

Summary

  • pouco (masculine, singular)
  • poucos (masculine, plural)
  • pouca (feminine, singular)
  • poucas (feminine, plural)

  • nós sabemos (we know)
  • vocês sabem (you know)

(audio 06:45)

O João está dentro de água.

João is in (inside) the water.

Ele vê um peixe ao lado dele e uma alga em cima da água.

He sees a fish next to him and (a) seaweed on the water.

Ele também vê uma luz dentro de água. Ele nada até à luz.

He also sees a light in (inside) the water. He swims to the light.

A luz é muito brilhante, mas está muito longe.

The light is very bright, but it is very far away.

Ele quer ir para cima, mas não consegue ir.

He wants to go up, but he can’t (go).

Há muitas caravelas-portuguesas na superfície.

There are many Portuguese man o’ war on the surface.

Mas ele precisa de ar. Precisa de respirar.

But he needs air. He needs to breathe.

Ele sente uma coisa na perna e depois perde os sentidos.

He feels something in his (the) leg and then loses consciousness.

* * *

NOTES

O João está dentro de (1) água. Ele vê um peixe ao lado dele (2) e uma alga em cima da (3) água.

He sees a fish next to him and (a) seaweed on the water. João is in (inside) the water.

(1)

dentro de: inside

  • O gato está dentro da caixa. The cat is inside the box. (da = de + a)

(2)

ao lado de: next to

  • O gato está ao lado da árvore. The cat is next to the tree. (da = de + a)

‎(3)

em cima de: on, on top of (= em)

  • O gato está em cima da mesa. The cat is on the table. (da = de + a)

Ele também vê uma luz dentro de água. Ele nada até à (4) luz.

He also sees a light in the water. He swims to the light.

(4)

The verb nadar is a regular verb. When you swim to a location, the verb is followed by two prepositions (nadar até a).

  • A tartaruga nada até à praia. The turtle swims to the beach. (à = a + a)

The same prepositions (até a) are used with other movement verbs, such as andar (to walk), correr (to run), saltar (to jump).

  • Eu ando até à cozinha. I walk to the kitchen.
  • Eu corro até à estação. I run to the station.
  • Eu salto até ao teto. I jump to the ceiling.

A luz é muito brilhante, mas está muito longe (5).

The light is very bright, but it is very far away.

(5)

longe (de): far (from)

  • O gato está longe. The cat is far.
  • O gato está muito longe da casa. The cat is very far from the house. (da = de + a)

perto (de): close (to)

  • O gato está perto. The cat is close.
  • O gato está perto do carro. The cat is close to the car. (do = de + o)

Ele quer ir para cima (6), mas não consegue ir (7).

He wants to go up, but he can’t (go).

(6)

para cima: up (movement)

  • O gato vai para cima. The cat goes up.

‎(7)

conseguir (to be able to) + infinitive: can, to be able to do something, to manage to do something

The structure conseguir (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used when you are able to to something mentally or physically or when you achieve something.

The verb conseguir is an irregular verb in the first person singular (eu consigo).

  • Eu consigo fazer o exercício de matemática. I am able to do the math exercise.

conjugation of the verb ‘conseguir’ (present)

  • eu consigo (I can)
  • tu consegues (you can)
  • ele consegue (he can)
  • nós conseguimos (we can)
  • vocês conseguem (you can)
  • eles conseguem (they can)

Mas ele precisa de ar (8). Precisa de respirar (9).

But he needs air. He needs to breathe.

(8)

precisar (to need) + de + noun: to need something

The structure precisar (conjugated) + de + noun is used when you need something (noun). The verb precisar is a regular verb.

  • Eu preciso de pão. I need bread.
  • Eu preciso da chave. I need the key.

‎(9)

precisar (to need) + de + infinitive: to need to do something

The structure precisar (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used when you need to do something.

  • Eu preciso de comer. I need to eat.
