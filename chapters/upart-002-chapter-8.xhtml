CHAPTER 8

(audio 17:24)

O Ricardo e a Marina dão um passeio na praia.

Ricardo and Marina take a walk on the beach.

A Marina fica longe da água porque tem medo.

Marina stays away from the water because she is afraid.

É uma hora da tarde. Eles têm fome.

It’s one o’clock in the afternoon. They’re hungry.

— O que é isso? — pergunta o Ricardo.

“What is that?” asks Ricardo.

Ele aponta para o pescoço da Marina.

He points to Marina’s neck. (lit. to the neck of Marina)

— Isto? — pergunta a Marina a tocar no colar. — Isto é um colar.

“This?” asks Marina touching the necklace. “This is a necklace.”

A Marina não quer mostrar o colar.

Marina doesn’t want to show the necklace.

— Esse colar é muito bonito?

“Is that necklace very pretty?”

— Não. Este colar é muito simples. Só tem uma medalha.

“No. This necklace is very simple. It only has a medal.”

— Essa medalha é valiosa?

“Is that medal valuable?”

— Não, esta medalha só tem valor pessoal. Tenho fome. Vamos almoçar?

“No, this medal has only personal value. I’m hungry . Let’s have lunch?” (lit. I have hunger. We go have lunch?)

— Claro. Eu quero comer marisco e beber vinho branco! — diz ele.

“Sure. I want to eat seafood and drink white wine!” he says.

O Ricardo parece feliz, mas não está satisfeito.

Ricardo looks happy, but he’s not satisfied.

NOTES

É uma hora (1) da tarde (2). Eles têm fome.

It’s one o’clock in the afternoon. They’re hungry.

(1)

To say the time, you use the verb ser.

  • É uma hora. It’s one o’clock.
  • São duas horas. It’s two o’clock.
  • São três horas. It’s three o’clock.

‎(2)

To specify whether the time is in morning, the afternoon or the evening, you can use the following complements.

da manhã: in the morning

  • São duas da manhã. It’s two in the morning.

da tarde: in the afternoon

  • São duas da tarde. It’s two in the afternoon.

da noite: at night

  • São oito da noite. It’s eight at night.

— O que é isso (3)? — pergunta o Ricardo.

“What is that?” asks Ricardo.

(3)

You have seen the demonstratives isto, este and esta (this).

You will now learn isso (that). This demonstrative is used when the thing you are referring to is close to the person receiving the message but far away from the speaker. This is a generic demonstrative, used when you are not defining the noun in terms of gender and number. It’s common in the question What is that? and in the answer This is….

  • O que é isso? What is that (close to you)?
  • Isto é um livro. This is a book.

— Esse (4) colar é muito bonito?

“Is that necklace very pretty?”

(4)

esse: that (masculine, singular)

This demonstrative is used when what you are referring to is close to the speaker, and you are characterizing it in terms of gender and number. In this case, it refers to a masculine, singular noun.

  • Esse livro é azul. That book (close to you) is blue.

— Essa (5) medalha é valiosa?

“Is that medal valuable?”

(5)

essa: that (feminine, singular)

This demonstrative is used when what you are referring to is close to the speaker, and you are characterizing it in terms of gender and number. In this case, it refers to a feminine, singular noun.

  • Essa caneta é azul. That pen (close to you) is blue.
