* * *

NOTES

— Pode ficar (1) aqui.

“You can stay here.”

(1)

poder (conjugated) + infinitive: to have permission to do something, to have the opportunity to do something. The verb poder is an irregular verb.

  • Eu posso comer o chocolate. I can eat the chocolate.
  • Eu posso entrar aqui. I can enter here.‎

conjugation of the verb ‘poder’ (present)

  • eu posso (I can)
  • tu podes (you can)
  • ele pode (he can)
  • nós podemos (we can)
  • vocês podem (you can)
  • eles podem (they can)

ficar: to remain, to stay

The verb ficar is a regular verb and it can have different meanings. You can use it to identify a permanent location (similar to the verb ser), to say that something or someone remains in a certain way or location, among other meanings.

  • Lisboa fica em Portugal. Lisbon is in Portugal.
  • O João fica em casa. João stays at home.

conjugation of the verb ‘ficar’ (present)

  • eu fico (I stay)
  • tu ficas (you stay)
  • ele fica (he stays)
  • nós ficamos (we stay)
  • vocês ficam (you stay)
  • eles ficam (they stay)

— Queria (2) o menu, por favor.

“I’d like the menu, please.”

(2)

Queria means I would like. This is the verb querer (to want) conjugated in the imperfect (pretérito imperfeito). The imperfect is used to talk about the past, but you can also use it to ask polite requests. Queria literally translates as I would want but means I would like. For now, you can learn how to use two useful verbs in the imperfect that will help you make polite requests:

  • Eu queria…: I would like…
  • Podia…?: Could you…?

Instead of the imperfect, you can also use the conditional, but you don’t need to worry about that for now.

  • Eu queria um café. I would like a coffee.
  • Podia abrir a janela? Could you open the window?

— Desculpe (3). Conhece (4) o homem de barba?

“Excuse me. Do you know the man with the beard?”

(3)

desculpe: excuse me (formal). Remember that desculpe is also used to apologize formally (meaning sorry).

  • Desculpe, pode ajudar-me? Excuse me, can you help me?

(4)

conhecer: to know someone

  • Eu conheço o Papa. I know the Pope.

conjugation of the verb ‘conhecer’ (present)

  • eu conheço (I know)
  • tu conheces (you know)
  • ele conhece (he knows)
  • nós conhecemos (we know)
  • vocês conhecem (you know)
  • eles conhecem (they know)

‎Note: in the 1st person singular, the final -co changes to -ço to keep the same sound, otherwise it would sound like -ko.

— Os pratos são todos (5) bons.

“The dishes are all good.”

(5)

Similarly to muito and pouco, todo (all, very) is variable when placed before a noun. The ending changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.

  • feminine, singular: Eu quero toda a água. I want all the water.
  • masculine, singular: Eu quero todo o dinheiro. I want all the money.
  • feminine, plural: Eu vejo todas as cores. I see all the colors.
  • masculine, plural: Eu compro todos os livros. I buy all the books.

Summary

  • todo (masculine, singular)
  • todos (masculine, plural)
  • toda (feminine, singular)
  • todas (feminine, plural)

— Têm algum (6) prato de carne?

“Do you have any meat dishes?”

(6)

Similarly to muito, pouco and todo, algum (some, several, any - in a question) is variable when placed before a noun. The ending changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.‎

  • feminine, singular: Eu quero alguma água. I want some water.
  • masculine, singular: Eu quero algum dinheiro. I want some money.
  • feminine, plural: Eu vejo algumas cores. I see some colors.
  • masculine, plural: Eu compro alguns livros. I buy some books.

Summary

  • algum (masculine, singular)
  • alguns (masculine, plural)
  • alguma (feminine, singular)
  • algumas (feminine, plural)

— Não. Infelizmente não temos nenhum (7).

“No. Unfortunately, we don’t have any.”

(7)

Similarly to muito, pouco, todo and algum, nenhum (not any, none - used in a negative sentence) is variable when placed before a noun. The ending changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.

  • feminine, singular: Eu não quero nenhuma água. I don’t want any water.
  • masculine, singular: Eu não quero nenhum dinheiro. I don’t want any money.
  • feminine, plural: Eu não vejo nenhumas cores. I don’t see any colors.
  • masculine, plural: Eu não compro nenhuns livros. I don’t buy any books.

Summary

  • nenhum (masculine, singular)
  • nenhuns (masculine, plural)
  • nenhuma (feminine, singular)
  • nenhumas (feminine, plural)

* * *

NOTES

O homem continua a olhar (1) para ela.

The man continues to look at her.

(1)

continuar (to continue) + a + infinitive: to continue to do something

The structure continuar (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used to refer to an action that continues to happen. The verb continuar is a regular verb.

  • Eu continuo a estudar português. I continue to study Portuguese.

A Marina está distraída. Rapidamente (2), o homem de barba rouba a mala da Marina.

Marina is distracted. Quickly, the bearded man steals Marina’s bag.

(2)

Words ending in -mente are adverbs. You can transform adjectives into adverbs by adding the ending.

  • simples (simple) > simplesmente (simply)
  • final (final) > finalmente (finally)

If the adjective ends in -o, then you change the o to a.

  • curioso (curious) > curiosamente (curiously)
  • delicioso (delicious) deliciosamente (deliciously)

* * *

NOTES

Um homem de fato no restaurante tenta apanhar (1) o ladrão.

A man in a suit at the restaurant tries to catch the thief.

(1)

tentar (to try) + infinitive: to try to do something

The structure tentar (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used when you try to do something. The verb tentar is a regular verb.

  • Eu tento acabar o exercício. I try to finish the exercise.

O ladrão corre à frente do (2) homem de fato. O homem de fato corre rapidamente atrás dele (3).

The thief runs ahead of the man in the suit. The man in the suit runs quickly after him.

(2)

à frente de: in front of, ahead

  • O gato está à frente da casa. The cat is in front of the house. (da = de + a)
  • O rato corre à frente do gato. The mouse runs ahead of the cat. (do = de + o)

(3)

atrás de: behind, after

  • O gato está atrás da árvore. The cat is behind the tree. (da = de + a)
  • O gato corre atrás do rato. The cat runs after the mouse. (do = de + o)

‎Summary

  • dentro de (= em) (inside, in)
  • em cima de (on)
  • ao lado de (next to)
  • à frente de (in front of)
  • atrás de (behind, after)
  • perto de (near)
  • longe de (far)

Um (4) minuto, dois minutos, três minutos, quatro minutos, cinco minutos.

One minute, two minutes, three minutes, four minutes, five minutes.

(4)

os números (the numbers)

  • um: 1
  • dois: 2
  • três: 3
  • quatro: 4
  • cinco: 5

‎Remember that the numbers um (1) and dois (2) change gender if they refer to a specific noun.

  • ‎um gato (one cat) / uma gata (one cat - female)
  • dois gatos (two cats) / duas gatas (two cats - female)

— Peço desculpa (5). Mas o ladrão corre muito depressa.

“I apologize. But the thief runs very fast.”

(5)

Peço desculpa is another way to apologize formally. Here are the common ways to apologize.

  • desculpa (informal): I am sorry
  • desculpe (formal): I am sorry
  • Peço desculpa (formal): I apologize

‎Note that in the expression peço desculpa, desculpa is a noun (a desculpa: the apology). For this reason, the word desculpa is invariable. Don’t confuse this noun with the verb desculpar that is used for the other two apologies in this list.

— Oh não... — diz a Marina. — O passaporte e o resto do dinheiro estão no hotel. Não tenho mais (6) dinheiro aqui.

“Oh no…” says Marina. “The passport and the rest of the money are at the hotel. I have no more money here.”

(6)

mais: more

  • ‎Eu quero mais água. I want more water.

‎menos: less

  • ‎Eu quero menos água. I want less water.

— Eu posso ajudar. — diz o homem de fato. — Eu sou o Ricardo. E sou polícia. Muito prazer (7).

“I can help,” says the man in a suit. “I’m Ricardo. And I’m a policeman. Nice to meet you.”

— Igualmente (8).

“Likewise.”

(7)

muito prazer: nice to meet you (lit. much pleasure)

(8)

igualmente: likewise (the reply to muito prazer)

  • ‎— Eu sou a Susana. I am Susana.
  • — Muito prazer. Nice to meet you.
  • — Igualmente. Likewise.

Section 5.4

(audio 11:24)

— É turista? — pergunta o Ricardo.

“Are you a tourist?” asks Ricardo.

— Sim. — responde a Marina.

“Yes,” answers Marina.

— Há quanto tempo está em Portugal?

“How long have you been in Portugal?”

— Estou em Portugal há um dia.

“I’ve been in Portugal for a day.”

— Só há um dia? Então, ainda não conhece nada.

“Only for a day? So, you still haven’t seen anything.” (lit. you still don’t know anything)

— É verdade.

“That’s right.” (lt. it is true)

— Eu posso mostrar as atrações amanhã. — oferece ele.

“I can show (you) the attractions tomorrow,” he offers.

— Obrigada, mas eu tenho um encontro amanhã.

“Thank you, but I have a meeting tomorrow.”

— Compreendo.

“I understand.”

— O encontro é na ponta de Sagres. Se calhar podemos dar um passeio depois. — diz a Marina.

“The meeting is at (the) Sagres Point. Maybe we can go for a walk later,” says Marina.

— Adoraria.

“I would love that.”

* * *

NOTES

— Há quanto tempo (1) está em Portugal?

“How long have you been in Portugal?”

(1)

Há quanto tempo means how long.

  • Há quanto tempo aprendes português? How long have you been learning Portuguese?

‎Note that this structure uses the present tense in Portuguese (lit. How long do you learn Portuguese?).

— Estou em Portugal há (2) um dia.

“I’ve been in Portugal for a day.”

(2)

The structure há + time means for (period of time).

  • Eu aprendo português há um ano. I have been learning Portuguese for a year.

— Só há um dia? Então, ainda não conhece (3) nada.

“Only for a day? So, you still haven’t seen anything.” (lit. you still don’t know anything)

(3)

The verb conhecer can be used to refer to a person you have met and also a location you have visited before.

  • Eu conheço Lisboa. I know Lisbon.

— Obrigada, mas eu tenho um encontro (4) amanhã.

“Thank you, but I have a meeting tomorrow.”

(4)

ter um encontro (com): to have a meeting/date (with)

  • Eu tenho um encontro com uma amiga. I have a date with a friend.

— O encontro é na ponta de Sagres. Se calhar (5) podemos dar um passeio (6) depois. — diz a Marina.

“The meeting is at (the) Sagres Point. Maybe we can go for a walk later,” says Marina.

(5)

se calhar: maybe

  • Se calhar, a chave está na gaveta. Maybe the key is in the drawer.

(6)

dar um passeio: to go for a walk. The verb dar is an irregular verb (it literally means to give).

  • Eu dou um passeio no parque todos os dias. I go for a walk every day in the park.

conjugation of the verb ‘dar’ (present)

  • eu dou (I give)
  • tu dás (you give)
  • ele dá (he gives)
  • nós damos (we give)
  • vocês dão (you give)
  • eles dão (they give)
