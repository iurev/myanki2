Section 7.1

(audio 14:00)

NOTES

Ele tem um descapotável grande (1) e vermelho.

He has a big red convertible. (lit. a convertible big and red)

(1)

grande: big

Adjectives ending in -e are not variable according to gender. They only change according to the number of the noun they refer to (adding -s).

  • O carro grande. The big car.
  • Os carros grandes. The big cars.
  • A casa grande. The big house.
  • As casas grandes. The big houses.

— Bom dia, Ricardo. Sim, estou pronta. Ah! O dia está ótimo (2)!

“Good morning, Ricardo. Yes, I’m ready. Oh! The day is great!”

(2)

To talk about how the weather is on a particular day, you use the verb estar for momentary characteristics.

  • Está calor. It’s hot. (lit. heat)
  • Estão dez graus. It’s ten degrees.

— Sim, está bom (3). Está calor e não está vento. E estão vinte (4) graus.

“Yes, it’s good. It’s hot and not windy. And it’s twenty degrees.”

(3)

bom: good

This adjective changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.

  • masculine, singular: O dia bom. The good day.
  • feminine, singular: A comida boa. The good food.
  • masculine, plural: Os dias bons. The good days.
  • feminine, plural: As canetas boas. The good pens.

mau: bad

This adjective also changes according to the gender and number of the noun it refers to.

  • masculine, singular: O dia mau. The bad day.
  • feminine, singular: A comida má. The bad food.
  • masculine, plural: Os dias maus. The bad days.
  • feminine, plural: As canetas más. The bad pens.

(4)

numbers 0-20

  • 0: zero
  • 1: um
  • 2: dois
  • 3: três
  • 4: quatro
  • 5: cinco
  • 6: seis
  • 7: sete
  • 8: oito
  • 9: nove
  • 10: dez
  • 11: onze
  • 12: doze
  • 13: treze
  • 14: catorze
  • 15: quinze
  • 16: dezasseis
  • 17: dezassete
  • 18: dezoito
  • 19: dezanove
  • 20: vinte

— Parece verão (5).

“It feels (seems) like summer.”

(5)

as estações do ano: the seasons

  • a primavera: the Spring
  • o verão: the Summer
  • o outono: the Fall/Autumn
  • o inverno: the Winter

— E é primavera. O tempo está bom para (6) ir à praia.

“And it’s spring. The weather is fine to go to the beach.”

(6)

estar bom para: to be good to/for‎. Note that the preposition para indicates a destination or a goal.

  • Hoje está bom para nadar. Today is good for swimming.

— Pois. Mas eu não gosto de nadar (7). Eu não gosto do mar (8).

“Right. But I don’t like to swim. I don’t like the sea.”

(7)

gostar (to like) + de + infinitive: to like to do something

The structure gostar (conjugated) + de + infinitive of the main verb is used to say that you like doing something. The verb gostar is a regular verb.

  • Eu gosto de desenhar. I like to draw.

(8)

gostar (to like) + de + noun: to like something

The structure gostar (conjugated) + de + noun is used to say that you like something.

  • Eu gosto de chocolate. I like chocolate.

— Não? Impossível! Eu adoro nadar (9).

“No? Impossible! I love to swim.”

(9)

adorar (to love) + infinitive (or noun): to love to do something / to love something

‎The structure adorar (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used to say that you like doing something. The verb adorar is a regular verb (it literally means to adore).

  • Eu adoro marisco. I love seafood.

— Na verdade, não sei nadar (10). Tenho medo da água (11).

“Actually, I can’t swim (I don’t know how to swim). I’m afraid of the water.”

(10)

saber (to know) + infinitive: to know how to do something

The structure saber (conjugated) + infinitive of the main verb is used to say that you know how to do something (an activity that you learned).

  • Eu sei cozinhar. I know how to cook. (I can cook.)

(11)

ter medo de: to be afraid of

  • Ele tem medo de cães. He is afraid of dogs.
  • Eu tenho medo de cair. I am afraid to fall.

Section 7.2

(audio 15:16)

Eles chegam à ponta de Sagres.

They arrive at (the) Sagres Point.

A Marina tem um encontro com o faroleiro.

Marina has a meeting with the lighthouse keeper.

— Conheces o faroleiro? — pergunta o Ricardo.

“Do you know the lighthouse keeper?” Ricardo asks.

— Sim. Ele é um velho amigo da família. Esperas aqui?

“Yes. He’s an old friend of the family. Are you going to wait here?”

— Sim, fico aqui. Eu gosto de ver o mar. — responde ele.

“Yes, I’ll stay (remain) here. I like to watch the sea,” he replies.

— Até já.

“See you soon.”

O Ricardo observa o mar, mas também observa a Marina.

Ricardo watches the sea, but he also watches Marina.

Ela abraça o faroleiro e os dois falam.

She hugs the lighthouse keeper, and the two talk.

* * *

NOTES

— Sim. Ele é um velho amigo (1) da família. Esperas aqui?

“Yes. He’s an old friend of the family. Do you wait here?”

(1)

As you have seen before, you place the adjective after the noun.

  • A casa grande. The big house.

However, the adjective can also be placed before the noun. It makes it more emphatic, and it can also change its meaning.

Compare:

  • um amigo velho: an old friend (a friend that is old)
  • um velho amigo: an old friend (a dear friend, a friend that has been a friend for a long time)
  • um homem grande: a big man
  • um grande homem: a great man

— Sim, fico (2) aqui. Eu gosto de ver o mar. — responde ele.

“Yes, I’ll stay (remain) here. I like to see the sea,” he replies.

(2)

You can see that the verb ficar has many meanings.

In this section of the story, it means to remain, to stay. You can use it to refer to the location of something or someone that remains in a specific location or state.

  • Eu fico em casa. I stay at home.

— Até já (3).

“See you soon.”

(3)

There are several different ways to say goodbye:

  • até já: see you soon
  • até logo: see you later
  • até amanhã: see you tomorrow
  • até à próxima: see you next time
  • tchau: bye (informal)
  • adeus: goodbye

(audio 16:13)

— Há quanto tempo! Como estás, Marina? — pergunta o faroleiro José.

“Long time no see! How are you, Marina?” asks the lighthouse keeper José.

* * *

NOTES

— Há quanto tempo! Como estás (1), Marina? — pergunta o faroleiro José.

“Long time no see! How are you, Marina?” asks the lighthouse keeper José.

(1)

Here is how you can ask how someone is doing.

  • Como estás?: How are you? (informal)
  • Como está? How are you? (informal)

‎If you need to specify whom you are talking to, or if the circumstance is especially formal, you can add the pronoun:‎

  • Como está o senhor? How are you? (to a man)
  • Como está a senhora? How are you? (to a woman)

Como (how) is an interrogative pronoun.

  • Como vais para casa? How are you going home?

— Bem (2), obrigada. E tu (3)?

“Well, thank you. And you?”

(2)

bem: well

This is a common way to answer when someone asks how you are doing. Another variation is muito bem (very well).

mal: poorly

  • Eu vejo bem sem óculos. I see well without glasses.
  • Eu sinto-me mal. I feel bad/poorly.

‎(3)

e tu?: and you?

If you want to ask the same question formally, you can say:

  • E o senhor? And you? (to a man)
  • E a senhora? And you? (to a woman)

If the circumstance is not especially formal, you can substitute the pronoun with the person’s name.

  • E o Pedro? And you, Pedro?
  • E a Ana? And you, Ana?

— O que é isto (4)?

“What is this?”

(4)

You will now learn how to identify something in terms of its location, in relation to the speaker and the receiver of the message. For this, you will place a word (demonstrative) before the noun.

The demonstrative isto (this) is used when the thing you are referring to is close to the speaker. This is a generic demonstrative, used when you are not defining the noun in terms of gender and number. It’s common in the question What is this? and in the answer This is….

  • O que é isto? What is this?
  • Isto é um livro. This is a book.

— Esta (5) medalha guarda um segredo. Qual é o segredo? Eu não sei... Este (6) segredo é complicado.

“This medal holds a secret. What is the secret? I don’t know... This secret is complicated.”

(5)

esta: this (feminine, singular)

This demonstrative is used when what you are referring to is close to the speaker, and you are characterizing it in terms of gender and number. In this case, it refers to a feminine, singular noun.

  • Esta caneta é azul. This pen is blue.

‎(6)

este: this (masculine, singular)

This demonstrative is used when what you are referring to is close to the speaker, and you are characterizing it in terms of gender and number. In this case, it refers to a masculine, singular noun.

  • Este livro é azul. This book is blue.
