NOTES

O João (1) é (2) biólogo (3).

João is a biologist.

(1)

When you are referring to someone by using their proper name, the name is preceded by the definite article (the).

  • o for a man’s name (masculine article): o Pedro, o Francisco, o Jorge, o João
  • a for a woman’s name (feminine article): a Ana, a Carla, a Cláudia, a Antónia

‎‎(2)

The verb ser is an irregular verb used to express defining/permanent characteristics, such as identifying someone’s profession or occupation.

  • O Pedro é advogado. Pedro is a lawyer.

conjugation of the verb ‘ser’ (present)

  • eu sou (I am)
  • tu és (you are)
  • ele é (he is)
  • nós somos (we are)
  • vocês são (you are)
  • eles são (they are)

The list above includes all the conjugations but doesn’t show all the personal pronouns to save space.

Note that the 2nd person plural (vocês) and 3rd person plural (eles) always have the same conjugation.

Remember that the 3rd person includes:

  • singular > ele (he), ela (she), formal
  • plural > eles (they, masculine), elas (they, feminine), formal

‎(3)

The ending o (biólogo) usually identifies professions performed by a man. There are other endings, but let’s focus on this one for now.

  • O Carlos é médico. Carlos is a doctor.

A Antónia é reformada (4).

Antónia is retired.

(4)

The ending a (reformada) usually identifies a profession performed by a woman.

  • A Filipa é enfermeira. Filipa is a nurse.

Compare:

  • O Carlos é médico. Carlos is a doctor.
  • A Ana é médica. Ana is a doctor.
  • O José é enfermeiro. José is a nurse.
  • A Filipa é enfermeira. Filipa is a nurse.

O João é primo da Antónia (5).

João is Antónia’s cousin.

(5)

The preposition de means of/from.

  • O Carlos é de Portugal. Carlos is from Portugal.

‎The preposition de contracts (merges) with the following word when it is an article.

  • de + a Antónia = da Antónia (lit. of the Antónia)
  • de + o Pedro = do Pedro (lit. of the Pedro)
  • A Joana é prima do André. Joana is André’s cousin.
  • O João é primo da Antónia. João is Antónia’s cousin.

O João está (6) em (7) Portugal (8).

João is in Portugal.

(6)

The verb estar is irregular and it’s used to express a momentary characteristic or a current state, such as identifying someone’s location.

conjugation of the verb ‘estar’ (present)

  • eu estou (I am)
  • tu estás (you are)
  • ele está (he is)
  • nós estamos (we are)
  • vocês estão (you are)
  • eles estão (they are)
  • ‎‎O Joaquim está em França. Joaquim is in France.

‎‎(7)

The preposition em means in, on, at, and it is used to identify the location.

  • A Ana está em Inglaterra. Ana is in England.

‎‎(8)

You have learned that proper names can have articles. In the case of proper names, it’s easy to know which are feminine (women) and which are masculine (men). But every other noun (be it an object or an abstract idea) is also either masculine or feminine. This also includes countries and cities.

  • masculine: o livro (the book), o Porto (Oporto), o Canadá (Canada)
  • feminine: a cadeira (the chair), a zebra (the zebra), a Alemanha (Germany)

You will learn to identify if a noun is masculine or feminine, but there are exceptions. For this reason, it is always a good idea to learn the word’s gender every time you learn a new one.

When it comes to countries, some don’t need an article. You need to learn which countries need one and which don’t.

Examples without an article:

  • Portugal (Portugal)
  • Angola (Angola)
  • Cuba (Cuba)
  • Marrocos (Marocco)
  • São Tomé e Príncipe (São Tomé and Príncipe)
  • Moçambique (Mozambique)
  • Timor-Leste (East Timor)

Examples with an article:

  • a África do Sul (South Africa)
  • a Alemanha (Germany)
  • a Austrália (Australia)
  • a Bélgica (Belgium)
  • o Brasil (Brazil)
  • o Canadá (Canada)
  • o Chile (Chile)
  • a China (China)
  • o Egito (Egipt)
  • (a) Espanha (Spain)
  • (a) França (France)
  • a Grécia (Greece)
  • a Índia (India)
  • (a) Inglaterra (England)
  • a Irlanda (Ireland)
  • a Itália (Italy)
  • o Japão (Japan)
  • o México (Mexico)
  • o Quénia (Kenya)
  • o Reino Unido (United Kingdom)
  • a Rússia (Russia)
  • a Turquia (Turkey)

Note: The articles between brackets mean that they are optional.

Ele está nos Açores (9).

He is in the Azores.

(9)

You have seen before that the preposition em means in, on or at. The same way that you contract the preposition de (of, from) with the article that follows (if there is one), you will do the same with the preposition em.

  • em + a Alemanha = na Alemanha (in Germany)
  • em + o Brasil = no Brasil (in Brazil)

The articles (o, a), that you can see in the examples above, identify masculine and feminine nouns (a Alemanha, o Brasil). These nouns are singular, i.e., they refer to one thing.

If you are referring to more than one thing, you will have a plural article and a plural noun.

  • singular: o livro (the book), a cadeira (the chair)
  • plural: os livros (the books), as cadeiras (the chairs)

You can see in the examples above that an -s was added to both the article and the noun to form the plural.

‎Summary of definite articles

  • masculine: o (singular), os (plural)
  • feminine: a (singular), as (plural)

‎‎The contraction between preposition and article will happen whether the noun is singular or plural.

Summary of the contractions

(of the, from the)

  • de + o = do
  • de + a = da
  • de + os = dos
  • de + as = das

(in the, on the, at the)

  • em + o = no
  • em + a = na
  • em + os = nos
  • em + as = nas

The Azores is a plural noun; it refers to a group of islands (os Açores).

  • Ele está nos Açores. He is in the Azores. (nos = em + os)

Here are a few examples of countries that are plural nouns:

  • os Estados Unidos da América (USA)
  • as Filipinas (Philippines)
  • os Países Baixos (Netherlands)

Os Açores são (10) em Portugal.

The Azores are in Portugal.

(10)

Besides being used to express defining/permanent characteristics, the verb ser is used to identify a permanent location.

  • Lisboa é em Portugal. Lisbon is in Portugal.

‎Compare:

  • defining characteristic: A Antónia é reformada. Antónia is retired.
  • permanent location: Os Açores são em Portugal. The Azores are in Portugal.

Ele está em casa (11) da Antónia.

He is at Antonia’s (house).

(11)

The noun casa can have two meanings:

  • a casa: the house
  • casa (without an article): home
  • ‎Ele está em casa. He is at home.

E ele está de férias (12).

And he is on vacation.

(12)

The structure estar de férias means to be on vacation.

  • O Pedro está de férias. Pedro is on vacation.

— Olá, João.

Hello, João.

— Oh, não! O vinho tinto não está aqui.

‎Aqui está o vinho branco.

“Oh, no! The red wine is not here.

I am distracted and I am tired. But, no problem!

Here is the white wine.”

* * *

NOTES

— Olá (1), João (2).

Hello, João.

(1)

The greeting olá (hello, hi) is a common informal greeting.

(2)

When speaking directly to the person (direct speech), you don’t need the article before the person’s name (o João). You only need the article when you are talking about them, not directly to them.

O vinho tinto não está aqui. (3)

The red wine is not here.

(3)

To build a negative sentence, you place the adverb não (no) before the verb.

  • ‎O vinho tinto não está aqui. The red wine is not here.

Eu sou (4) distraída e estou (5) cansada.

I am distracted and I am tired.

(4)

The verb ser is an irregular verb used to express defining characteristics. You can use it to identify someone’s profession or occupation. As you can see in this example, you can also use it to describe someone’s personality traits.

  • Eu sou paciente. I am patient.

(5)

The verb estar is an irregular verb used to express momentary characteristics. You can use it to identify someone’s location. As seen in this example, you can also use it to describe someone’s current physical or emotional state.

Eu estou triste. I am sad.

Mas não há problema (6)!

But, no problem!

(6)

The expression não há problema is a common way to say no problem.

  • Pode ajudar-me? Can you help me?
  • Claro, não há problema! Sure, no problem!

Aqui (7) está o vinho branco (8).

Here is the white wine.

(7)

The word aqui means here. Whatever you are talking about is physically close to you. Note that the location can be placed at the beginning or at the end of the sentence.

  • O vinho está aqui. The wine is here.
  • Aqui está o vinho. Here is the wine.

(8)

The adjective branco (white) is placed after the noun vinho (wine).‎ In Portuguese, the adjective is usually placed after the noun.

  • A casa branca. The white house.
  • O livro preto. The black book.

Note that, as with the professions, the adjective’s ending agrees in gender and number with the noun it refers to.

  • A casa branca. (feminine, singular) > As casas brancas. (feminine, plural)
  • O livro preto. (masculine, singular) > Os livros pretos. (masculine, plural)
