* * *

NOTES

Ele está a ver (1) o mar.

He is watching the sea.

(1)

estar (to be) + a + infinitive: to be doing something

The structure estar (conjugated) + a + infinitive of the main verb is used to talk about an ongoing action.

  • Eu estou a comer. I am eating.
  • Tu estás a comer. You are eating.
  • O Pedro está a comer. Pedro is eating.
  • Nós estamos a comer. We are eating.
  • Vocês estão a comer. You are eating.
  • O Tiago e a Daniela estão a comer. Tiago and Daniela are eating.

— O jantar está pronto! Tu (2) estás com fome (3)?

“Dinner is ready! Are you hungry?”

(2)

Tu is used to talk to a person informally. It’s usually used with close family members, friends, children, and co-workers the same age as you or that you work closely with.

(3)

A yes-no question (a question with an answer that is either yes or no) follows the same structure as an affirmative sentence. You simply need to change the intonation. Compare:

  • Tu estás com fome. You are hungry.
  • Tu estás com fome? Are you hungry?

(audio 01:50)

* * *

NOTES

O João olha para (1) o mar. Ele chama (2) a Antónia.

João looks at the sea. He calls Antónia.

(1)

Some verbs are followed by prepositions, such as olhar para (to look at). The preposition para, here, indicates the direction.

  • O Pedro olha para a Maria. Pedro looks at Maria.

(2)

You have seen some irregular verbs in action, such as ser and estar.

You will now learn how to conjugate regular verbs. These are verbs that follow a specific conjugation pattern. This means that if you know the verb’s infinitive, you are able to determine the conjugation for each personal pronoun.

There are three types of regular verbs: verbs ending in -ar, -er and -ir.

To conjugate the verb, you need to remove the ending from the infinitive (either -ar, -er or -ir) and add the ending for that particular personal pronoun (eu, tu, ele, etc.).

In this story section, we have two verbs that end in -ar: olhar (to look), chamar (to call).

We can see that the conjugation for the third person singular (ele, ela, formal) ends in -a:

  • O João olha… (João looks)
  • Ele chama… (He calls)

If I give you a new verb ending in -ar, you’ll be able to conjugate it in the third person singular (ele, ela, formal). All you need to do is drop the ending -ar and add -a.

The verb ‘falar’ (to speak)

  • Ele fala português. He speaks Portuguese.

The verb ‘comprar’ (to buy)

  • Ele compra vinho. He buys wine.

Here is the ending for each personal pronoun after you remove the ending -ar from the infinitive.

conjugation of the verb ‘chamar’ (present)

  • eu chamo (I call)
  • tu chamas (you call)
  • ele chama (he calls)
  • nós chamamos (we call)
  • vocês chamam (you call)
  • eles chamam (they call)

— Ali (3)! Uma coisa (4) no mar. Uma ilha?

“There! Something (a thing) in the sea. An island?”

(3)

You have seen before that aqui means here. Whatever you are talking about is physically close to you.

When you use the word ali, whatever you are talking about is physically far away from you, and from the person you are talking to (for example: What is that ‘over there’?)

  • O Pedro está ali. Pedro is there.
  • O Pedro está aqui. Pedro is here.

(4)

You have previously learned the definite article used to refer to specific nouns (o, a, os, as).

  • o mar: the sea
  • o vinho tinto: the red wine
  • a Ana: (the) Ana
  • a França: (the) França
  • os Açores: the Azores
  • as Filipinas: the Philippines

You’ll now learn the indefinite article, which refers to something in generic terms (for example, in English, a car, an ant). As with the definite articles, you will have masculine and feminine, and singular and plural indefinite articles.

  • o carro (the car) > um carro (a car)
  • a cadeira (the chair) > uma cadeira (a chair)
  • os carros (the cars) > uns carros (cars)
  • as cadeiras (the chairs) > umas cadeiras (chairs)

The plural indefinite articles (uns, umas) usually means a few/some, so you can leave it out of the sentence if that’s not what you wish to express.

  • Eu olho para uns carros. I look at some cars.
  • Eu olho para carros. I look at cars.

Summary of the articles

  • masculine: definite (o, os) / indefinite (um, uns)
  • feminine: definite (a, as) / indefinite (uma, umas)

— Onde? Eu não vejo (5) nada (6).

“Where? I don’t see anything.”

(5)

The verb ver (to see, to watch) is an irregular verb.

  • Eu vejo o mar. I see the sea.

conjugation of the verb ‘ver’ (present)

  • eu vejo (I see)
  • tu vês (you see)
  • ele vê (he sees)
  • nós vemos (we see)
  • vocês veem (you see)
  • eles veem (they see)

(6)

Nada (nothing, anything) is used in a negative sentence and placed after the verb.

  • Ele não olha para nada. He doesn’t look at anything.

Que estranho. Agora (7) o João também não (8) vê a coisa.

That’s weird. Now João also doesn’t see the thing.

(7)

The word agora (now) can be placed before the personal pronoun or at the end of the sentence.

  • Eu estou cansado agora. I am tired now.
  • Agora eu estou cansado. Now I am tired.

(8)

The word também (also, as well) is placed between the personal pronoun and the verb.

  • Eu também sou professor. I am also a teacher.

Compare também (also, as well) with também não (also not, neither).

  • Eu também sou alto. I am also tall.
  • Eu também não sou alto. I am also not tall.
