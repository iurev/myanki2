OVERVIEW OF THE LANGUAGE

Here is an overview of how the Portuguese language works. The goal of this section is not to learn or memorize this information but to give you a general idea of what is to come.

If you want an overview before you start, this is for you.

If you are an adventurer and prefer to discover the language features as you move through the story, please do so and skip this part! You can always come back if you need to.

Let’s start with a simple sentence. I will break it down for you, explaining the different features.

  • Eu como um bolo grande de chocolate.
  • I eat a big chocolate cake.
  1. subject: eu (I)
  2. verb: como (eat)
  3. noun: um bolo (a cake)
  4. adjective: grande (big)
  5. preposition: de chocolate (of chocolate)
  6. type of sentence: how to turn the sentence into a question

1. the subject

The subject is the person acting.

You can say, John is cooking (where ‘John’ is the subject), and you can also say, He is cooking. He is the subject, and it is also a personal pronoun, a word in place of the name.

Here is the list of personal pronouns in Portuguese:

SINGULAR

1st person:

  • eu (I)

2nd person:

  • tu (you, informal)

3rd person:

  • ele (he)
  • ela (she)

formal (you) (*)

  • o senhor (when talking to a man)
  • a senhora (when talking to a woman)
  • você (generic, usually left out of the sentence) (**)

PLURAL

1st person:

  • nós (we)

2nd person:

  • vocês (you, informal)
  • vós (you, informal) (***)

3rd person:

  • eles (they, referring to men or a mixed group)
  • elas (they, referring to women)

formal

  • os senhores (when talking to men or a mixed group)
  • as senhoras (when talking to women)

Note (*): the formal treatment is under the 3rd person. Although you are talking directly to someone, you use the verb conjugation for ele (he), ela (she).

Note (**): você is used as a semi-formal pronoun. This means that the situation is not particularly formal; you probably already know the person, but you still need to be polite and somewhat distant. Because this pronoun might be perceived as rude, depending on the situation, and you are still getting started, I recommend that you don’t use it for now. All you need to do is drop the pronoun from the sentence, and you’re good to go! The verb alone works perfectly well. You’ll learn more about this in the book.

Note that você is used in Brazil informally (similar to tu in Portugal).

Note (***): vós, once the rule when speaking to more than one person informally, is now used only in certain regions by the older generations. You might still see it in grammar books and verb conjugation charts. Vocês is now the standard.

Informal vs. formal treatment

You can address a person formally or informally when speaking directly to them.

In simple terms, you will speak formally to anyone you’ve never met before (at a store, restaurant, etc.). You may also use the formal treatment when talking to someone you know in various circumstances. For example, speaking with someone older, with someone with whom you have a professional relationship, or simply people with whom you don’t have a close relationship.

You will usually speak informally with family members, close friends, children and young people, and also co-workers, depending on your relationship.

2. the verb

The verb represents the action (what happens). The verb in the example sentence is comer (to eat). The word comer is the infinitive; that’s the word you’ll find in a dictionary; it’s the unaltered version of the verb.

Once you have someone acting, the verb needs to transform, i.e., it needs to be conjugated. The conjugation is the transformation you do to the infinitive according to the person acting.

The conjugation of the verb happens by changing the ending of the infinitive. Some verbs are regular (you can apply the same pattern of endings to any regular verb), and some are irregular (they don’t follow specific rules, so you need to learn each conjugation separately).

Apart from this, there are three types of verbs depending on the ending of the infinitive. You have verbs ending in:

  • -ar (such as falar - to speak)
  • -er (such as comer - to eat)
  • -ir (such as partir - to break)

Example of a regular verb ending in -er, conjugated in the present (the action happens now):

  • comer (to eat)
  • eu como (I eat)
  • tu comes (you eat)
  • ele, ela, formal come (he eats)
  • nós comemos (we eat)
  • vocês comem (you eat)
  • eles, elas, formal comem (they eat)

Verbs also have different verb tenses and modes. They express the time or how the action takes place.

Example:

  • eu comi (I ate)
  • eu comeria (I would eat)

3. the noun

A noun is a word that identifies something (either physical or abstract, such as house, cat, or love). In Portuguese, every noun has a gender; it can either be masculine or feminine.

For example:

  • homem (man) is a masculine noun
  • mulher (woman) is a feminine noun

The examples above seem straightforward, but what about a chair or a book? Are they also either masculine or feminine? Yes, and you must learn if each noun is masculine or feminine.

For example:

  • cadeira (chair) is a feminine noun
  • livro (book) is a masculine noun

But don’t worry; the ending of the noun will help you identify the noun’s gender (almost always).

A noun can also be singular (one) or plural (more than one).

For example:

  • cadeira (chair) is a singular noun
  • cadeiras (chairs) is a plural noun

As you can see from the example above, a plural noun usually adds -s to the ending but there are other cases.

The noun’s gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural) also affect other words in the sentence that refer to the noun. This includes articles (such as the and an, in English) and other preceding words.

For example:

  • a cadeira (the chair) / o livro (the book)
  • uma cadeira (a chair) / um livro (a book)
  • essa cadeira (that chair) / esse livro (that book)
  • algumas cadeiras (some chairs) / alguns livros (some books)
  • outras cadeiras (other chairs) / outros livros (other books)
  • essas cadeiras (these chairs) / esses livros (these books)

As you can see from these examples, the noun’s gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) influence the form of the word preceding it.

4. the adjective

An adjective is a word that describes a noun; it characterizes and adds information about it. It also has to agree with the noun in terms of gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) as with the articles and other words we’ve seen before, i.e., it also changes its form accordingly.

For example

adjective pequeno (small):

  • o carro pequeno (the small car)
  • os carros pequenos (the small cars)
  • a caneta pequena (the small pen)
  • as canetas pequenas (the small pens)

The adjective is usually placed after the noun (unlike in English).

5. the preposition

A preposition is a word that connects nouns or nouns and pronouns. It can identify the location of something, and when or how something happens.

For example:

de (of):

  • O livro do gato. The book of the cat. (The cat’s book.)

debaixo de (under):

  • O livro debaixo do gato. The book under the cat.

sobre (about):

  • O livro sobre o gato. The book about the cat.

You’ve seen before that a noun can be preceded by an article, a little word that identifies a noun.

  • o gato (the cat)

In Portuguese, it’s common to contract a preposition with the article that follows (you turn both words into one word).

For example:

preposition de (of)

  • O livro do gato. The book of the cat. (do = de + o)

6. type of sentence

You can easily turn the example sentence into a yes-no question (a question that you can answer with either yes or no). All you need is the question mark at the end and a change in intonation.

  • Eu como um bolo grande de chocolate. I eat a big chocolate cake.
  • Eu como um bolo grande de chocolate? Do I eat a big chocolate cake?

An open question starts with an interrogative pronoun followed by the verb.

For example:

  • Quem é o homem? Who is the man?
  • Quanto custa o bolo? How much does the cake cost?
  • Onde está o livro? Where is the book?
